# Sustainability Model for BOSC Community Library

This document outlines a detailed funding strategy for the BOSC Community Library and compares two open source sustainability models: a service-based model inspired by Red Hat, and a foundation-driven model.

## Project Context

BOSC Community Library is a public-focused knowledge repository for community-centered wireless communication systems. The library aims to support interoperable, secure, and locally managed connectivity solutions for education, civic engagement, and digital inclusion.

## Funding Strategy Overview

A sustainable funding strategy for this library should be multi-channel and aligned with public-sector values.

Key funding sources:
- paid services and professional support for implementation, deployment, and training
- membership, sponsorship, and cooperative funding from public institutions and industry partners
- grant funding and government program support for open infrastructure
- contributor-driven maintenance funding and community stewardship

The recommended approach is a hybrid model: use service revenue to fund short-term operations while building a foundation-backed governance and contributor support structure for long-term sustainability.

## Model 1: Service-Based Model (Red Hat Style)

### Description

This model monetizes expertise and execution rather than the software itself. The library remains open source, while revenue is generated from:

- enterprise-grade support and maintenance agreements
- deployment services and integration into institutional networks
- training, documentation, and capacity-building workshops
- security audits and compliance certification support
- custom feature development for public-sector requirements

### Strengths

- predictable, revenue-generating offerings aligned with customer success
- direct relationship with clients and public-sector customers
- funding tied to tangible services, which can scale with demand
- minimal licensing friction for adopters

### Risks and Limitations

- dependence on a single service provider or consulting organization can create vendor concentration
- smaller public-sector budgets may prefer grant-funded or collaborative governance structures
- may not fully resolve perceptions of vendor lock-in if services are bundled with proprietary delivery practices

### Applicability to BOSC Community Library

For this library, a service-based model is especially strong for ministries and institutions that need:

- rapid deployment guidance for community network use cases
- hands-on support for compliance, security, and educational policy integration
- training for local technical staff and educators
- tailored implementations for non-profit and academic campus environments

This model can generate early funding while preserving open access to the library itself.

## Model 2: Foundation-Driven Model

### Description

A foundation-driven model places stewardship in a neutral, non-profit entity. Funding sources include:

- membership dues from public agencies, academic institutions, NGOs, and industry partners
- sponsorships from aligned vendors and technology partners
- grants from development agencies, education funds, and philanthropic programs
- donor contributions and targeted campaign funding for public-interest features
- collaborative maintenance pools and shared development initiatives

### Strengths

- governance focused on community benefit rather than a single commercial sponsor
- strong credibility for public-sector adoption and procurement
- lower perceived risk of vendor lock-in because decision-making is shared
- easier access to multi-stakeholder grants and inter-agency cooperation

### Risks and Limitations

- slower revenue generation and more complex governance overhead
- requires active community engagement and well-defined contributor processes
- sustainability depends on consistent member participation and funding diversity

### Applicability to BOSC Community Library

A foundation-driven model is valuable for this library when the goal is:

- long-term open infrastructure stewardship for education and civic technology
- transparent governance over feature roadmap and security practices
- wide participation from ministries, universities, and community organizations
- grant-backed development for public-interest priorities such as accessibility and localization

This model can provide structural stability and build trust among public-sector stakeholders.

## Comparative Summary

| Dimension | Service-Based (Red Hat) | Foundation-Driven |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue source | Support, consulting, training | Membership, sponsorships, grants, donations |
| Speed to revenue | Faster | Slower |
| Governance | Vendor-led | Community-led |
| Public-sector appeal | Practical and delivery-focused | Transparent and neutral |
| Lock-in risk | Higher if services are tightly coupled | Lower due to shared governance |
| Ideal for | Rapid deployment, implementation support | Long-term stewardship, standards, collaborative funding |

## Recommended Strategy for BOSC Community Library

For sustainability, the library should adopt a hybrid approach:

1. Launch service offerings that fund operations and help public-sector adopters deploy the library successfully.
2. Establish a governance body or foundation committee to oversee the library's core roadmap, licensing, and community stewardship.
3. Seek grants and sponsorships for public-interest features such as localization, accessibility, and curriculum-aligned resources.
4. Use service revenue to stabilize short-term operations while the foundation structure matures.
5. Maintain transparent reporting, a code of conduct, and a public roadmap to attract donors and institutional members.

## Practical Steps

- Define service packages for education-focused deployment, security review, and training.
- Create foundation bylaws, membership tiers, and governance principles aligned with public-sector values.
- Build a funding plan with a 12–24 month runway, combining service income, membership dues, and grant targets.
- Develop a public-sector partnership pitch that emphasizes reduced TCO, open standards, and local capacity building.
- Publish clear contributor and governance policies to reassure ministries and institutions.

## Conclusion

A mixed service + foundation model offers the best fit for BOSC Community Library. It enables the library to sustain ongoing development while preserving the open, neutral governance that public-sector education stakeholders demand.
